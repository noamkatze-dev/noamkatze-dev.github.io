## Context

Microsoft Clarity gives product teams heatmaps and session recordings. This project adds an automated layer on top: it flags manipulative UI patterns, known as dark patterns, in the behavioral data teams already collect.

It was an academic team project at the University of Haifa. We worked together across research, design, data analysis and presentation. [TODO: one sentence on how work was split and which part was mine]

## The problem

Optimizing on behavioral data can push teams toward experiences that exploit users' emotional or cognitive patterns, often unintentionally.

Spotting this today means manually combing through session recordings for rage clicks, repeated interactions and abandoned flows, across fragmented tools.

## Goal

Detect dark patterns early, at the analysis and decision stage, and reduce users' exposure to manipulative experiences.

## Research

**Interviews.** We interviewed [TODO: number] UX practitioners and analyzed industry workflows. Teams rely heavily on behavioral data to find usability issues, but investigating frustration signals is manual, time-consuming and fragmented. That pointed to the opportunity: centralize and automate detection inside Clarity, where the data already lives.

**Literature review.** We reviewed academic research that classifies dark patterns by category, severity and frequency, and translated it into measurable behavioral indicators that Clarity's existing capabilities can capture.

- They valued the AI-generated summaries.

**What didn't**

- Improvement tips lacked visual emphasis and were buried in the side panel.
- The participant expected explanations when hovering over pattern names.
- There was no way to hide patterns the user consciously chooses to ignore.
