## Microsoft Clarity

Microsoft Clarity gives product teams heatmaps and session recordings. This project adds an automated layer on top: it flags manipulative UI patterns, known as dark patterns, in the behavioral data teams already collect.

We worked as one team across research, design, data analysis and presentation. [TODO: one sentence on how work was split and which part was mine]

## The problem

Optimizing on behavioral data can push teams toward experiences that exploit users' emotional or cognitive patterns, often without meaning to.

Spotting this today means combing through session recordings by hand for rage clicks, repeated interactions and abandoned flows, across fragmented tools.

## The goal

Detect dark patterns early, at the analysis and decision stage, and reduce users' exposure to manipulative experiences.

## Research insights

**Interviews.** Through interviews with UX practitioners and analysis of industry workflows, we found that teams rely heavily on behavioral data to find usability issues. But investigating frustration signals is manual, time-consuming and fragmented across tools.

**Literature review.** We reviewed academic research that classifies dark patterns by category, severity and frequency, and translated it into measurable behavioral indicators that Clarity can already capture.

**The opportunity:** centralize and automate detection inside Clarity, where the data already lives. [TODO: number of interviews, and 1 to 2 concrete insights, ideally with a short paraphrased quote]

## Jobs to be done

- Understand the nature and context of each detected pattern.
- Prioritize by severity and decide what needs immediate action.
- Quickly spot problematic patterns in user behavior.
- Receive clear, actionable insights, not just charts or raw data.
- Shorten analysis time to support efficient decisions.

## Widget evolution

Four versions, from a score inside an existing tab to a dashboard built around session impact. Each one answered a problem the previous version exposed.

<Iteration>
  <IterationStage
    title="1 · Initial integration"
    video="dark-pattern-detection/iteration-1-ethical-insights"
    alt="Version 1: the Clarity dashboard with an Ethical Insights tab showing Total Ethical Flags 286, Average Ethical Score 52%, Critical Criteria 3 and Transparency Index 44%."
  >
    The widget lived inside an existing dashboard tab as “Ethical Insights”: the number of detected patterns, an overall score and a severity breakdown, plus an internal page with deeper graphs.
  </IterationStage>
  <IterationStage
    title="2 · A dedicated experience"
    video="dark-pattern-detection/iteration-2-dp-detect"
    alt="Version 2: the Clarity dashboard with a DP Detect widget listing Leaving cart, CTA rejection, Flow break, Newsletter skip and Leaving form."
  >
    It needed a stronger presence, so it moved to its own tab as “DP Detect”, highlighting the user actions each pattern triggered. An internal page explained the severity logic and listed every pattern with recordings and heatmaps.
  </IterationStage>
  <IterationStage
    title="3 · Centralized dashboard"
    video="dark-pattern-detection/iteration-3-centralized"
    alt="Version 3: a single Dark Patterns widget with 14 total and 6 critical dark patterns, a three-level severity bar, and the patterns sorted by most critical."
  >
    Two places meant fragmentation and navigation friction. We removed the internal page and brought everything into one dashboard: all detections, critical cases first, sorting by severity, and recordings and heatmaps in context.
  </IterationStage>
  <IterationStage
    title="4 · Session impact focus"
    video="dark-pattern-detection/iteration-4-session-impact"
    alt="Version 4: the Dark Patterns widget with 1,960 affected sessions, a checkbox per pattern, and an affected-sessions-over-time line chart with one line per pattern."
  >
    The final version shifts from detection to real impact: affected sessions per pattern, filtering and comparison, and a graph of impact over time. Recordings jump to the exact moment of impact.
  </IterationStage>
</Iteration>
