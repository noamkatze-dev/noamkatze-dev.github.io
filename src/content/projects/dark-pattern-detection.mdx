## Microsoft Clarity

Microsoft Clarity is a behavioral analytics platform: product teams use its heatmaps and session recordings to understand how people use their sites. Our team explored a new feature for it that flags potentially manipulative interface patterns, known as dark patterns, assesses their severity, and links each one to the sessions where it happened.

## Understanding Clarity first

Before designing anything new, I wanted to understand the product we were adding to. On my own initiative, I analyzed the entire Clarity platform in depth, before and during the design process:

- how the platform works, end to end
- its existing features and the workflows analysts follow
- where a dark-pattern detection feature could fit into the current product
- which existing capabilities could support it and help us develop the concept

I shared the findings with my teammates to inform our collective research, design decisions and feature development.

## The problem

Optimizing on behavioral data can push teams toward experiences that exploit users' emotional or cognitive patterns, often without meaning to.

Spotting this today means combing through session recordings by hand for rage clicks, repeated interactions and abandoned flows, across fragmented tools.

## The goal

Detect dark patterns early, at the analysis and decision stage, and reduce users' exposure to manipulative experiences.

## Research insights

**User interviews.** We interviewed UX practitioners and looked at how teams work with behavioral data. [TODO: number of interviewees] Teams rely heavily on recordings and interaction data to find usability issues, but investigating frustration signals such as rage clicks, repeated interactions and abandoned flows is manual, time-consuming and spread across tools.

**Literature review.** We reviewed academic research that classifies dark patterns by category, severity and frequency, and translated it into behavioral indicators that Clarity's existing capabilities can measure.

**The opportunity:** centralize and automate dark-pattern detection inside Clarity, where the data already lives.

## Jobs to be done

- Understand the nature and context of each detected pattern.
- Prioritize by severity and decide what needs immediate action.
- Quickly spot problematic patterns in user behavior.
- Receive clear, actionable insights, not just charts or raw data.
- Shorten analysis time to support efficient decisions.

## Key decisions

<Decisions>
  <Decision
    title="Build on signals Clarity already captures"
    choice="Translate the academic dark-pattern taxonomy into behavioral indicators Clarity can already measure, instead of designing new tracking."
    why="The platform analysis showed which existing capabilities could support detection, and the interviews showed that investigating frustration signals was manual and fragmented. Detecting patterns inside Clarity, on data teams already collect, addresses both."
  />
  <Decision
    title="Every pattern links straight to its recordings"
    choice="Each detected pattern opens the recordings where it happened, filtered to that pattern and summarized by AI."
    why="Finding the evidence by combing through recordings is the slowest part of the work. Linking each detection to its sessions removes that step."
  />
  <Decision
    title="One dashboard, not a dashboard plus an internal page"
    choice="We removed the separate internal page and moved severity sorting, recordings and heatmaps into a single dashboard."
    why="Splitting the feature across two places created fragmentation and navigation friction."
  />
  <Decision
    title="Measure impact, not just detection"
    choice="Each pattern shows how many sessions it affected, with filtering, comparison and a graph of impact over time."
    why="Analysts need to decide what to act on first. Showing impact over time connects detection to business insight."
  />
</Decisions>

## Usability test

After designing the wireframes, we ran a usability test with one participant to see how a user would react to the feature and move through it.

### What we found

<Checklist
  good={[
    "The participant understood the purpose of the feature right away.",
    "They moved from the dashboard to pattern details and the relevant session recordings without assistance.",
    "They valued the AI-generated insight summaries.",
  ]}
  bad={[
    "Recommendations lacked visual emphasis and were buried in the side panel.",
    "They expected an explanation when hovering over a dark pattern's name.",
    "There was no way to hide patterns they had chosen to ignore.",
  ]}
/>

### What we changed

1. **Recommendations moved into view.** Fix tips now appear when you hover a dark-pattern marker on the recording timeline, at the moment of the problem. The side panel stays for the full AI analysis.
2. **Explanations in context.** Hovering a pattern's name explains what the pattern is, so users don't have to leave the dashboard to understand it.
3. **Hiding ignored patterns.** Users can hide patterns they have consciously decided to ignore, which keeps the list focused on what still needs attention. [TODO: confirm this change made it into the final design, and where]

With a single participant, these findings are directional rather than conclusive.

## Reflection

I learned that identifying a problem is only part of the design process: the information also has to be presented in a way that helps people understand it and act on it. Looking back, I would test the feature with a broader range of users and scenarios.
