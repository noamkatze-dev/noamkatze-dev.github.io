## 250ms

What your eyes reveal before you've decided what you think.

An interactive experience about visual attention and political imagery. Using only a webcam, it follows where viewers look as political photographs appear, then visualizes their gaze paths, fixations and heatmaps, to explore how these images may shape the way we perceive reality. I designed and built it on my own.

## The question

Political imagery surrounds us daily, in the news, on social media and in campaigns, shaping how we perceive reality. We usually ask what people *think* about these images.

I wanted to ask a different question: how do we *look* at them? Our eyes choose where to land, what to linger on and what to avoid, before we've decided what we think.

## How it works

With nothing but a webcam and real-time facial-landmark detection, the system calibrates to each viewer's gaze, then estimates where they look while they view a set of political photographs, recording fixations, saccades and attention density.

Before any data is collected, the viewer completes a 9-point calibration, holding their gaze on each point while the system builds a personal map between iris position and screen coordinates.

The system records what the eyes do naturally: fixations, scanpaths, first glance and attention density. Age, gender and location are logged too, so viewers can be compared later.

Each region a viewer's gaze lingers on is mapped to a rough position on a left-right arc, based on who occupies that region. It is deliberately presented as an artistic, conceptual interpretation, not a scientific measurement, and the screen says so.

## Design system

The sky is where the eye rests between charged images. Clouds ask nothing of you, take no side and provoke no feeling. That innocence is the point: it is the exact opposite of looking at a political image, which is never neutral.

Over that calm backdrop sits a thin red grid and deep red type, the visual language of surveillance and lab instruments.

## Reflection

I learned how to translate a research-driven concept into an interactive experience about visual attention and political imagery. Webcam tracking is noisy by nature, which is why the experience needed a personal calibration and an adaptive filter. Looking back, I would validate the technical feasibility and accuracy of webcam-based eye tracking earlier in the process.
